Resolve the branch from the source before reading details. A human subject carries compositional or narrative focus through scale, isolation, action, gaze or placement; distant incidental passers-by alone do not qualify.

## Distilled expression

- **Posture:** emotion comes from pause, distance, back-facing orientation, slight turn and body-to-space relation rather than facial expression. For a new figure, let the scene's path, edge, support and sightline choose the action; a look-back is only the fallback.
- **Added body:** use a late-teen or young-adult youth or girl with simple contemporary clothing, always completely barefoot. Draw in pure-white coarse dry-pencil, chalk or fast-marker strokes: uneven broken contours, a few doubled or overshooting search lines, sparse directional hatching and large transparent gaps. Keep anatomy readable without polished garment, muscle or foot detail.
- **Bare feet:** express each foot with only a few rough marks—short ankle indication, loose foot contour and a small contact mark. Do not draw toes, toenails, soles, socks, shoes or sandals. The marks must connect to the real ground, roof, step, ledge, bench or shore.
- **Anonymous head:** cover the entire visible head with dense irregular, mostly-horizontal pure-white scribbles of mixed length and thickness. Slight overrun is welcome, but keep the bundle near the natural head scale so the neck and shoulder direction remain readable. No facial features, smooth disk or censor rectangle.
- **Contact:** make foot, pelvis, hand or forearm contact agree with visible ground, step, ledge, wall, rail or waterside geometry. Preserve gravity, weight, perspective and occlusion; never import a support that exists only in the reference.

Existing bodies remain protected; only their visible heads receive the shared anonymous-head language. Visible heads or a new figure footprint are authorized edit zones regardless of route or orientation.
